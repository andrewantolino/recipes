# Greek Marinated Chicken Thighs (Vacuum Bag Method)

Makes enough marinade for 1 kg of chicken thighs (about 6-7 thighs), split across a dinner batch and a freezer batch.

**Scaling rule:** this recipe is built around 1 kg of chicken. Double everything for 2 kg, halve everything for 500 g.

## Ingredients (for 1 kg chicken)

- 1 kg chicken thighs (about 6-7), bone-in or boneless
- 90 ml olive oil
- 12 g garlic, minced (about 4 cloves)
- 7 g dried oregano (about 2 tbsp)
- 6 g salt (about 1 tsp)
- 1 g freshly ground black pepper (about 1/2 tsp)
- 3 g smoked paprika (about 1 tsp)
- 5 g Dijon mustard (about 1 tsp)
- 15 g fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped (a small handful)
- 1 lemon, sliced (added directly to the vac bags, not juiced into the marinade)

**Optional addition:**
- 15 ml red wine vinegar (1 tbsp)

**Per 100 g of chicken (for odd amounts):** 9 ml olive oil, 1.2 g garlic, 0.7 g oregano, 0.6 g salt, 0.1 g pepper, 0.3 g smoked paprika, 0.5 g Dijon, 1.5 g parsley. Use 1 lemon per kg.

## Method

1. **Make the marinade.** In a large bowl, whisk together the olive oil, garlic, oregano, salt, pepper, smoked paprika, Dijon and parsley (plus red wine vinegar if using).
2. **Coat the chicken.** Add all the chicken thighs to the bowl and toss to coat evenly in the marinade.
3. **Transfer to vacuum bags.** Divide the chicken and marinade between bags as needed, a dinner batch and a freezer batch. Divide the lemon slices between the bags before sealing.
4. **Seal.** Vacuum seal each bag. The vacuum helps press the marinade into the meat faster than an open bowl.
5. **Dinner batch:** marinate in the fridge for 1.5 hours, then cook.
6. **Freezer batch:** freeze promptly after sealing (don't let it sit in the fridge for a day or two first. Garlic in oil in a sealed, low-oxygen bag left in the fridge for extended periods is a botulism risk; freezing removes this concern). Keeps well for 1-2 weeks. Thaw in the fridge before cooking, which also acts as a final marinating stage.

## Cooking

- Grill, pan-sear, or oven-roast at 200°C until internal temperature reaches 74°C, with nicely charred/crisp skin if bone-in.
- Rest briefly before serving.
- Serve with a Greek salad, tzatziki, warm pita, or rice.

## Notes

- Don't marinate longer than 24 hours *before freezing* if using lemon juice, as the acid can start to "cook" and toughen the meat. This isn't a concern here since you're using lemon slices rather than juice, and the freezer batch will be frozen well within that window anyway.
- **Fridge life:**
  - Raw marinated chicken in a vacuum bag: cook the same day (ideally within a few hours). Because of the garlic-in-oil botulism risk, don't hold it in the fridge for days. If you won't cook it within about 24 hours at the very most, freeze it.
  - Thawed freezer batch: cook within 24 hours of thawing, and don't refreeze it raw.
  - Cooked leftovers: 3-4 days in an airtight container in the fridge (at or below 4°C), or freeze for up to 2-3 months.
- Freezing halts any acid effect entirely, so the freezer batch is safe for the full 1-2 weeks.
- Conversion basis: 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 tsp = 5 ml. Dried oregano is ~3.5 g per tbsp, and garlic is ~3 g per clove.
