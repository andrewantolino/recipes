# Pork Mince Noodles

A hybrid between lo mein and chow mein: boiled chow mein noodles tossed through crispy pork mince, seared mushrooms and crunchy veg in a soy, oyster and shaoxing sauce. Makes about 4 serves.

**Scaling rule:** this recipe is built around 500 g of pork mince and 400 g of noodles. Halve everything for 250 g. For 1 kg, double everything, but brown the pork in two batches so it fries instead of steams.

## Ingredients (for 500 g mince)

**Pork and veg**

- 500 g pork mince
- 2 medium carrots (about 200 g), sliced into thin coins
- 1 capsicum (about 200 g), sliced into thinnish strips
- 200 g mushrooms (shiitake and larger brown ones), thinly sliced
- 3 cloves garlic (about 9 g), minced
- 2 cm piece of ginger (about 10 g), finely grated or minced
- 4 spring onions (white and green parts separated), thinly sliced
- 2-3 tbsp neutral oil (for frying)
- 2 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 1 tsp cornflour whisked into 2 tbsp cold water (slurry)
- 1-2 tsp chilli oil (more to taste)
- 1 tsp sesame oil

**Sauce**

- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1-2 tbsp hoisin sauce (optional, adjust to taste)
- 100 ml water or stock
- 1/4 tsp white pepper

**Noodles**

- 400 g chow mein noodles
- 1 tsp neutral oil (to toss the cooked noodles)

## Method

1. **Cook the noodles.** Submerge the noodles in boiling water for 1-2 minutes (or per packet instructions), agitating to separate. Strain, rinse under cold water to stop the cooking, and drain really well. Toss with 1 tsp neutral oil so they don't clump, and set aside.
2. **Mix the sauce.** Combine the soy, oyster sauce, hoisin (if using), white pepper and water or stock in a small bowl.
3. **Brown the pork.** Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large wok or frying pan over high heat. Add the pork and break it up roughly. Leave it to fry undisturbed until it sizzles loudly and the base is crisp, then turn and repeat. You're after crispy edges. Remove to a bowl.
4. **Sear the mushrooms.** Add another 1 tbsp oil if the pan is dry. Add the mushrooms in a single layer (in two batches if needed) and leave them alone until golden. Salt lightly once browned.
5. **Add the carrots.** Add the carrots and stir-fry for 2-3 minutes, until starting to soften but still with some bite.
6. **Add the capsicum.** Add the capsicum and cook for about 1 minute, so it stays crisp.
7. **Add the aromatics.** Push the veg aside, add a little oil if needed, then add the garlic, ginger and spring onion whites. Stir for 20-30 seconds, until fragrant but not browned.
8. **Deglaze and sauce.** Return the pork to the pan. Pour the shaoxing wine around the edge of the pan so it sizzles, and let it cook off for about 20 seconds. Add the sauce mix and bring to a simmer.
9. **Thicken.** Re-stir the slurry (the cornflour settles to the bottom of the cup), pour it into the simmering sauce, and stir for about 1 minute until the sauce turns glossy and clings to the pork and veg.
10. **Toss with noodles.** Add the noodles and toss until evenly coated and heated through, 1-2 minutes. Add the sesame oil and chilli oil off the heat. Taste and adjust.
11. **Finish.** Top with the spring onion greens.

## Serving

- Serve straight from the pan while hot.
- Top with sliced fresh chilli, crispy shallots, or extra chilli oil.
- A drizzle of extra hoisin or a squeeze of lime works if you want it sweeter or brighter.

## Notes

- **Don't crowd the pan.** Pork and mushrooms steam instead of browning if the pan is too full. Cook in batches if needed.
- **Salt the mushrooms late.** Salting early draws out water and stops them browning.
- **Add the hoisin gradually.** It's sweet, so start with 1 tbsp and adjust at the end.
- **Add the chilli oil late.** Its flavour fades with heat, so stir it through at the end.
- **Crispy noodle variation:** pan-fry a handful of the cooked noodles in a little oil for a minute or two before the final toss, and pile the rest on top for extra texture.
- **Name:** zhajiangmian (fried-sauce noodles) is the closest traditional dish, especially if you lean on the hoisin.
- **Fridge life:** keeps 3 days in an airtight container at or below 4°C. Reheat until piping hot throughout.
- Quantities are estimates for a 500 g batch; adjust the sauce to taste.
- Conversion basis: 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 tsp = 5 ml.
