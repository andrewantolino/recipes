# Pork and Beef Ragu

A slow-simmered mince ragu with deeply browned meat, a wine and milk deglaze for tenderness, and a long, gentle cook. Makes about 8 serves.

**Scaling rule:** this recipe is built around 1 kg of mince (500 g pork + 500 g beef). Halve everything for 500 g (use 350 ml passata). For 2 kg, double everything, but brown the meat in batches and expect a longer simmer (see Notes).

## Ingredients (for 1 kg mince)

- 500 g pork mince
- 500 g beef mince
- 2 large onions (about 400 g), finely diced
- 12 g garlic, minced (about 4 cloves)
- 75 ml olive oil (about 5 tbsp: 2 tbsp for the onion, 3 tbsp for the meat)
- 150 ml red wine (optional)
- 250 ml full-cream milk
- 2 x 400 g cans crushed tomatoes
- 700 ml passata (1 bottle)
- About 200 ml water (to swish out the cans and bottle)
- 1 tsp salt for the onion, plus more to taste at the end
- 1 g freshly ground black pepper (about 1/2 tsp)
- 15 g fresh parsley, chopped (a small handful)
- 15 g fresh basil, torn (a small handful)

**Per 100 g of mince (for odd amounts):** 15 ml wine, 25 ml milk, 80 g crushed tomatoes, 70 ml passata, 40 g onion, 1.2 g garlic, 7 ml olive oil, 1.5 g each of parsley and basil.

## Method

1. **Sweat the onion.** Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a large, heavy pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion and salt generously. Cook for 10-12 minutes (a few minutes longer if needed), stirring occasionally, until soft and translucent.
2. **Add the garlic.** Stir in the garlic and cook for 3-5 minutes, until fragrant but not browned.
3. **Brown the mince.** Turn the heat to high on your largest burner. Add the pork and beef and break it up roughly. Leave it to fry undisturbed until it starts to sizzle loudly, then turn, break up, and repeat. You're building colour: the mince should caramelise deeply and stick to the base of the pot.
4. **Add oil.** Once there's a good fond on the base, add 3 tbsp olive oil, toss, and fry a little longer.
5. **Deglaze with wine (if using).** Pour in the red wine, scrape up the fond, and simmer until mostly evaporated (a few minutes).
6. **Deglaze with milk.** Pour in the milk and scrape up everything stuck to the bottom. Cook until the milk has been absorbed and the mince tastes tender and delicious.
7. **Season and add tomato.** Add a crack of black pepper. Add the crushed tomatoes and passata. Swish about 200 ml water total through the cans and bottle to get all the tomato residue, and add it to the pot.
8. **Bring to the boil.** Stir well and bring to a boil.
9. **Simmer, partly covered.** Move to your smallest burner and cook on medium-low with the lid partly on, stirring intermittently, for 1 hour.
10. **Simmer, uncovered.** Remove the lid and continue to cook, stirring intermittently, to reduce. Expect 2-2.5 hours total for this volume, until the sauce is thick, glossy, and the oil is pooling at the edges.
11. **Finish.** Stir in the parsley and basil. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper.

## Serving

- Toss through pappardelle, tagliatelle, or rigatoni, with a splash of pasta water to loosen.
- Also great in lasagne, over polenta, or on baked potatoes.
- Finish with grated Parmesan and a drizzle of good olive oil.

## Notes

- **Don't crowd the pot.** Mince steams instead of browning if the pot is too full. If your pot isn't wide enough to brown 1 kg well, brown it in two batches and combine before adding the wine.
- **Fat levels vary.** If your mince is very lean, add the extra oil as written. If it renders a lot of fat, you can skim some off before adding the wine.
- **Salt in stages.** Salting the onion seasons the base, but the sauce reduces a lot, so wait until the end for the final adjustment.
- **Add the herbs late.** Fresh parsley and basil lose their brightness over a long simmer, so stir them in at the end. Dried oregano or a bay leaf can go in with the tomatoes if you like.
- **Cook time:** go by the cues (thick, glossy, oil pooling) rather than the clock. Up to 3 hours is fine for a deeper, jammier sauce. If it's reducing too fast, put the lid back on partly or add a splash of water.
- **Acidity:** if the sauce tastes sharp at the end, a pinch of sugar or a small knob of butter will round it out.
- **Fridge life:** keeps 4 days in an airtight container at or below 4°C. Cool it quickly (spread it in shallow containers) before refrigerating.
- **Freezer life:** freezes well for 3 months. Portion into flat bags or containers, and thaw overnight in the fridge. Reheat until piping hot throughout.
- Conversion basis: 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 tsp = 5 ml. Garlic is ~3 g per clove.
