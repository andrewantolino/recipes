# Korean-Inspired Bang Bang Chicken (Sandwich Batch)

Cover-baked shredded chicken breast in a gochujang and peanut butter marinade.
Made as a batch with no mayo so it keeps well, then mixed into a sandwich
filling with mayo and spring onion when you're ready to eat. Makes about 8
sandwiches.

**Scaling rule:** this recipe is built around 1 kg of chicken breast. Double
everything for 2 kg, halve everything for 500 g.

## Ingredients (for 1 kg chicken)

### Chicken and marinade

- 1 kg chicken breast (about 4 breasts)
- 40 g gochujang (about 2 tbsp)
- 35 g smooth peanut butter (about 2 tbsp)
- 15 ml soy sauce (1 tbsp)
- 15 ml sesame oil (1 tbsp)
- 15 ml rice vinegar or lime juice (1 tbsp)
- 5 g fresh ginger, grated (about 1 tsp)
- 6 g garlic, minced (about 2 cloves)
- 30-50 ml warm water, to loosen into a spreadable paste
- Optional: 5 g honey or maple syrup (1 tsp) for extra sweetness

### Sandwich filling (per sandwich, about 120 g chicken)

- 120 g shredded chicken
- 15 g mayo (about 1 tbsp), Kewpie if you have it
- 1 spring onion, thinly sliced
- Optional: 5-10 g smooth peanut butter (1-2 tsp), loosened with the mayo
- Optional: a squeeze of lime, a drizzle of chili oil or chili crisp

**Per 100 g of chicken (for odd amounts):** 4 g gochujang, 3.5 g peanut butter,
1.5 ml soy, 1.5 ml sesame oil, 1.5 ml rice vinegar. For the filling, per 100 g
of chicken: 12 g mayo, 1 spring onion, 4-8 g extra peanut butter.

### Sandwich extras (to taste)

- Bread of choice, toasted or soft rolls
- Sliced cucumber, shredded cabbage or lettuce
- Fresh coriander
- Pickled carrot or quick-pickled cucumber

### Optional salad version

- Dressing: 70 g peanut butter, 30 ml lime juice, 30 ml soy sauce, 10 ml
  sesame oil, 10 g honey, 40-60 ml warm water to thin
- Salad: 300 g shredded cabbage, 150 g julienned carrot, 250 g cucumber
  batons, 4 spring onions, 20 g coriander, 40 g crushed peanuts, 1 tbsp
  sesame seeds

## Method

### Make the chicken

1. **Preheat.** Heat the oven to 180°C (fan-forced 160°C).
2. **Make the marinade.** In a bowl, whisk together the gochujang, peanut
   butter, soy sauce, sesame oil, rice vinegar, ginger, garlic and honey (if
   using). Add warm water a little at a time until it's a thick but
   spreadable paste.
3. **Coat the chicken.** Place the chicken breasts in a baking dish and rub
   the marinade over all sides.
4. **Cover and bake.** Cover tightly with a lid or foil. Bake for 35-45
   minutes, until the internal temperature reaches 74°C. For easier shredding,
   take it to 80°C or a few minutes beyond.
5. **Rest.** Leave the chicken covered in the dish for 10 minutes so it
   reabsorbs its juices.
6. **Shred.** Transfer the chicken to a stand mixer with the paddle
   attachment and mix on low until shredded (about 20-30 seconds).
   Alternatively, use two forks.
7. **Toss in some of the juices.** Return the shredded chicken to the dish
   and toss it in the pan juices a few tablespoons at a time, stopping when
   the meat is moist but not pooling. Save the rest (see Notes).
8. **Cool and store.** Let it cool, then portion into airtight containers or
   freezer bags (see Notes for storage).

### Make a sandwich

1. **Mix the filling.** In a bowl, combine the shredded chicken, mayo, spring
   onion and extra peanut butter (if using). Taste and add lime or chili oil
   if you like.
2. **Assemble.** Pile onto bread with cucumber, cabbage or lettuce, and
   coriander.

## Notes

- **Skip the extra salt.** Gochujang and soy are both salty, so taste before
  adding any.
- **Leftover juices:** the liquid left in the dish is concentrated flavour
  (chicken juice, gochujang, peanut butter, soy), so keep it. Too much added
  back makes the filling wet, soggy in bread, and shorter-lived, so only
  return what the chicken absorbs. Ways to use the rest:
  - **Reduce to a glaze:** simmer in a small pan for 5-10 minutes until
    syrupy. Stir it into the mayo for a sandwich spread, or drizzle it over
    rice or noodles.
  - **Make a sauce:** thin with a splash of water or lime juice, and add a
    little peanut butter if you want it creamier.
  - **Use as a broth base:** add stock and noodles for a quick
    peanut-gochujang soup.
  - **Storing it:** chill it and lift off the set fat if you like (or leave
    it in for richness). Keeps 3-4 days in an airtight container at or below
    4°C, or freeze in ice cube trays for 2-3 months and drop a cube into
    fried rice, soup or a sandwich filling. Cool it promptly before storing.
  - **Too much every time?** Chicken breast releases a lot of water when
    covered. Use the low end of the marinade water, or skip it if the
    gochujang and peanut butter loosen enough on their own.
- **Mix the filling per serving.** Mayo, spring onion and extra peanut butter
  all shorten shelf life or get soggy, so only mix what you'll eat that day.
  The plain chicken is the long-life part.
- **Fridge life:** plain shredded chicken (no mayo or spring onion) keeps 3-4
  days in an airtight container at or below 4°C. Cool it promptly before
  refrigerating. Mixed filling is best eaten the same day, or within 24 hours
  at the most.
- **Freezer life:** for a longer shelf life, freeze the plain chicken in flat
  bags of about 250 g (enough for 2 sandwiches). It keeps 2-3 months. Thaw
  overnight in the fridge and use within 2 days.
- **Packing lunches:** if you take a sandwich to work, mix the filling in the
  morning and keep it cold, or pack the chicken and mayo separately and
  combine just before eating, to stop the bread going soggy.
- **Extra peanut butter:** the baked-in amount gives a subtle background
  nuttiness. Adding 1-2 tsp to each filling gives a stronger hit and also
  thickens the mayo.
- **Yoghurt option:** thick Greek yoghurt can replace the mayo 1:1 for a
  lighter, tangier filling. Add a pinch of salt or sugar to balance it.
- **Splitting the flavour:** for a lighter bake, use just the gochujang, soy,
  sesame oil and ginger in the marinade, and add all the peanut butter in the
  filling.
- **Garlic:** the marinade is cooked straight away, so the garlic-in-oil
  storage concern doesn't apply. If you marinate ahead, keep it in the fridge
  for a few hours at most, or freeze it.
- Conversion basis: 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 tsp = 5 ml. Peanut butter is ~17 g per
  tbsp, gochujang ~20 g per tbsp, mayo ~15 g per tbsp.
